Predicting a player's next-round score

Research on refining the EuroGuru model · 10 November 2026

I tested 19 models plus blends. The best three beat the current EuroGuru model on prediction error in every season tested, by about 2–4%. A larger gain isn't available: a player's score swings by about 8.5 FPT from game to game and the best model's error is 7.0, so most of what remains is luck.

How it was tested

  • Data: every regular-season game of 2024-25 and 2025-26, plus this season's 10 rounds. 2023-24 has no game rows in the bucket, so it couldn't be used.
  • Scores: official FPT this season. For the older seasons FPT was rebuilt as PIR plus 10% for winners, which matches the official number on 99.5% of this season's rows.
  • Target: what he scores in his club's next game, counting 0 if he doesn't play.
  • Method: each round is predicted using only earlier games, from round 4 on.
  • Who is scored: players who played at least one of their club's last 10 games.
  • Squad test: the best 10 players for 97 CR (105 less a typical coach), captain ×2 and bench ×0.5, scored on what actually happened. It leaves the coach out, which is why the current model shows about 126 here rather than the 134 of the original backtest.

The three best models

1. Blend of 8 models Most accurate

  • Averages the predictions of eight models: ridge regression, the two-stage linear and tree models, minutes × points per minute, plain boosted trees, random forest and two neural networks.
  • Each model makes different mistakes, and averaging cancels some of them out.
  • Best or near-best in every season, and the only model whose squad gain is outside the noise.
  • More honest projections: its squads came in about 6 under projection in 2025-26, against about 12 for the current model.
  • Needs LightGBM and PyTorch in the backend to ship.

2. Two-stage trees Best single model

  • Answers two questions with gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM), then multiplies them: the chance he plays, and what he scores if he does.
  • Chance he plays is driven mostly by whether he played the last game (42% of the signal) and his current streak (20%).
  • Score if he plays is driven mostly by his season average (31%), then recent form, his median over the last 10 games, his club's win chance (winners get +10%), price and minutes.
  • Over-rates its top picks, so its squads were no better than the current model's.

3. Two-stage linear Easiest to ship

  • The same two questions, answered with a logistic regression for the chance he plays and a ridge regression for the score.
  • It is two weighted formulas, so it could run in the backend with numpy alone.
  • Error matches the trees in two of three seasons. Its squads beat the current model's by 5.7 a round in 2025-26, though that is within the noise.

What to keep in mind

  • The current model is already good. It beats season average, Your formula and last-5 by a wide margin; the new models improve on it only modestly.
  • This season's squad result is open. The accuracy gain held up in 2026-27, the squad gain did not, and 7 rounds can't settle it.
  • Things that didn't help: teammate-absence features, the Kalman skill tracker, robust loss functions, and recalibrating the current model.
  • Not testable: injuries. The bucket holds only today's injury list, so no model could learn from past ones.
  • Nothing in the app has changed. If one is shipped, the two-stage linear model is the natural first step: most of the gain, no new dependencies.
